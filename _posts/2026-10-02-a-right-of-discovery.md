The phrase “the doctrine of discovery” never appears in the 1823 *Johnson v. McIntosh* ruling. Chief Justice John Marshall wrote instead of “the right of discovery,” which he traced to England’s 1496 commission to John Cabot. Steven T. Newcomb reads the Latin Letters Patent, which directed the Cabots to find lands of “Heathens and Infidels” and to subjugate, occupy, and possess them, and draws on Burke Aaron Hinsdale’s 1888 article on the subject.
